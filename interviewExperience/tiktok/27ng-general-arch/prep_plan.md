# TikTok Prep Plan — 2 weeks, BQ + spoken English priority

> Reply to Suki/Mengya first: express interest, ask about the process (number of rounds, whether there's an OA), and schedule the first round ~2 weeks out. That buys you a defined runway.

## Guiding principle

Your technical stories are already strong on paper (see `BQ/bq_formal.md` — the OSS bucket story even has a spoken version). The gap you named is **speaking fluency**, so the plan is built around one rule:

**Every prep activity happens out loud.** No silent reading, no writing-only sessions. If your mouth isn't moving, it doesn't count as BQ/English prep.

## Daily English routine (60–75 min, every day, non-negotiable)

| Block | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Shadowing | 15 min | Pick a 3–5 min clip of a native engineer speaking (conference talk, mock interview on YouTube). Play a sentence, pause, repeat it imitating rhythm and stress. Same clip for 3 days before switching — repetition builds muscle memory. |
| Story reps | 20 min | Take ONE story from the question bank. Record yourself answering (phone voice memo). Listen back. Note filler words ("uh", "like", "you know") and Chinese-English gaps. Re-record until one clean take ≤ 90 seconds. |
| Follow-up sparring | 15 min | After the clean take, answer 3 follow-up questions on the same story out loud, unrehearsed. This trains the improvised speech that fails first under pressure. |
| Read-aloud tech vocab | 10 min | Read one section of your own resume or a system design answer out loud. Goal: your own technical terms ("decentralized", "idempotent", "throughput", "prioritization") should come out without stumbling. Keep a list of words you trip on; drill those 10 times each. |

Fluency techniques to apply in every rep:

- **Pause instead of filler.** A 1-second silence sounds thoughtful; "uhhh" sounds nervous. Practice literally closing your mouth between sentences.
- **Slow down 20%.** Non-native speakers rush when nervous. Slower = clearer accent, more thinking time, more senior-sounding.
- **Short sentences.** Chinese-to-English speakers build long nested sentences and get lost. One idea per sentence. Full stop. Next idea.
- **Signpost transitions**: "The situation was…", "The trade-off was…", "What I'd do differently is…" — memorized connectors keep you moving when your brain lags.
- **Don't restart sentences.** If you misspeak, keep going. Restarting is a fluency killer and interviewers barely notice small errors.

**Day 1–2: stories on paper → stories in mouth**

- Write and memorize the **30-second elevator pitch**: name → Duke MS ECE '27 → 3 internships all on AI-agent infrastructure (Microsoft research, Alibaba Cloud platform, BoulderAI applications) → what excites you about AI-native engineering infra.

**Day 3–4: project deep-dive fluency**
- Go through question bank sections A–E out loud. For each project, produce: a 2-minute overview take + answers to every listed follow-up.
- Where you don't know an answer (e.g., lock contention numbers), decide NOW what you'll honestly say — "I didn't measure that, but here's how I would" is a great answer; freezing is not.

**Day 5–7: technical layer**
- Coding: LRU (+ thread-safe variant), rate limiter, LFU — implement from scratch, then 5 LC Hot 100 mediums/day, **narrating your thinking in English the whole time**. The narration is the point; solving silently trains the wrong skill.

## Ongoing after round 1

- After every real round, write down every question you got (add to this folder — same habit as your other 面经 folders) and re-drill anything that went badly before the next round.
- Keep the daily English routine running between rounds; fluency decays in days.

## Measuring progress (so you know it's working)

- Week 1 baseline: record "tell me about yourself" on day 1. Don't delete it.
- Day 7 and day 13: re-record the same answer. Compare filler-word count and whether you stayed under 35 seconds.
- Target: any core story on demand, ≤ 90s, ≤ 2 fillers, no restarts.
